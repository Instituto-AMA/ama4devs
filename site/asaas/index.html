Asaas, on localhost.

The Asaas API v3, the Brazilian payment gateway, simulated with contract and behavioral fidelity: customers, charges with Pix, boleto and credit card, installment plans, subscriptions, refunds, splits, subaccounts, payer notifications and a virtual clock. Unlike the real sandbox, it delivers webhooks to localhost.

https://api-sandbox.asaas.com/v3
http://localhost:45445/v3
docker run --rm -p 45445:45445 ghcr.io/instituto-ama/asaas-mock:latest

The API key is printed at boot. Switching your application from the sandbox to the mock is switching the base URL.

Why not just use the Asaas sandbox

The sandbox does not deliver webhooks to localhost. That forces you into a tunnel, a staging deploy or hand-forged payloads, and none of them tests what matters: the credit delay, the overdue interest, the split, the webhook retry. And on the sandbox, a card payment credits in 32 real days.

Here time is yours:

# A credit card payment sits at CONFIRMED; the money lands at D+32.
curl -X POST localhost:45445/_admin/clock/advance \
     -H 'content-type: application/json' -d '{"days": 32}'
# → PAYMENT_RECEIVED arrives at your endpoint. It took 40 ms.

What it simulates

This is not a mock of static responses.

  • A state machine per billing type. Pix goes straight to RECEIVED; boleto credits at D+1 business day; credit card at D+32; debit at D+3. An unpaid charge passes through OVERDUE first.
  • Real money. netValue = value − fee, the fee is debited in the ledger, and the statement reconciles with the balance (a tested invariant).
  • Interest and late fees. The fine once, on the first day overdue; interest pro rata die over a 30-day commercial month.
  • Split across accounts. Every API key is an account with its own walletId and balance. A split moves money and posts entries to both statements.
  • Webhooks the way Asaas sends them. asaas-access-token header, only HTTP 200 counts (201 and 204 are failures, the real rule), 15-attempt backoff, SEQUENTIALLY with real head-of-line blocking, and interrupted at the end.
  • Real card handling. Tokenization (the PAN is never stored), test cards, pre-authorization and capture, chargeback. Card installments charge the card.
  • Recurrence. A subscription creates its first charge on creation; the next ones are born 40 days before their due date. In an installment plan the rounding remainder goes to the last installment, and on a card each one credits at D+32×n. (All three captured from the real sandbox, all three contradict what the docs suggest.)
  • Pix that actually pays. GET /v3/payments/{id}/pixQrCode returns a real EMV BR Code with CRC16 and the QR image: a phone camera reads it.
  • Subaccount onboarding. Create the subaccount, list the pending KYC documents, upload the file (multipart), approve it.
  • The whole API surface. Every operation of the official specification exists, with authentication, validation and the Asaas error format. Those without a business engine answer 501 naming the operation, never fake data (GET /_admin/coverage).

Payer notifications

What Asaas tells the person who pays, delivered for real on your machine:

  • Every charge event (created, updated, due soon, overdue, received) sends the payer an e-mail to Mailpit and a WhatsApp template through the WhatsApp mock's Cloud API.
  • An installment plan is told once: the first installment's creation tells the whole plan (R$ 3.000,00 em 5x de R$ 600,00); reminders and payment notices stay per installment.
  • Everything comes up configured: on boot and on every tick the sender number gets this server's webhook and the payer templates are created in the WhatsApp mock, through Meta's own calls.
  • The customer's notificationDisabled and notification settings are honored, a failing channel never blocks the charge, and the panel resends on demand.

The panel

Open http://localhost:45445. It is not the Asaas dashboard: it is the simulator's, and it shows what Asaas will not let you see.

  • Customers: create, edit, remove and restore; open one to see everything billed to it.
  • Charges, Subscriptions, Installment plans, and New charge with the three kinds Asaas offers (single, installments by total or per installment, subscription), any billing type, interest, fine, discount and split. Simulate sale opens the same form.
  • A charge: its invoice link, simulate the payment, confirm a cash receipt, receive, overdue, refund, delete, restore, and its notifications.
  • Integrations, per account: API keys (generate, rotate, expire on the virtual clock), webhooks (with the event checklist, through the real /v3/webhooks), webhook logs with your endpoint's answer, and the e-mail and WhatsApp channels.
  • Test cards: the table the engine reads; accept any well-formed card or only the listed ones; add a card with the outcome it forces.
  • Profiles: one root account per tenant of your application, created and deleted without a restart.

Use it in your project

services:
  asaas:
    image: ghcr.io/instituto-ama/asaas-mock:0.6.4   # pin a version, or :latest
    ports:
      - '45445:45445'
    environment:
      # Pin the key, otherwise a fresh volume generates a new one.
      # `$$` is a literal `$` in compose: a single `$aact_...` would become an empty variable.
      ASAAS_API_KEY: '$$aact_hmlg_local'
      # Inside the container localhost is the container: this sends webhooks to your machine.
      WEBHOOK_LOCALHOST_REWRITE: host.docker.internal
      # Time flows, and you can skip ahead (POST /_admin/clock/advance).
      CLOCK_MODE: VIRTUAL_FLOWING
    extra_hosts:
      - 'host.docker.internal:host-gateway'   # Linux
    volumes:
      - asaas-data:/data
volumes:
  asaas-data:

If your application runs in the same compose network, it reaches the mock at http://asaas:45445/v3. In CI, drop the rewrite and use CLOCK_MODE=VIRTUAL_FROZEN: the clock only moves when the test says so.

curl localhost:45445/v3/customers \
  -H 'access_token: $aact_hmlg_local' -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"name":"Jane Doe","cpfCnpj":"24971563792","email":"jane@example.com","mobilePhone":"11987654321"}'

To get the payer notifications, run the WhatsApp mock and Mailpit next to it and set WHATSAPP_MOCK_URL and MAILPIT_URL (the root docker-compose.yml does it all).

Payment control routes

Key-free helper routes mounted under /_mock give deterministic control over payment states in automated tests and local verification: list, confirm, receive, overdue, fail (an alias for overdue) and refund (full or partial).

curl http://localhost:45445/_mock/payments?limit=10
curl -X POST http://localhost:45445/_mock/payments/pay_123456789012/confirm
curl -X POST http://localhost:45445/_mock/payments/pay_123456789012/receive
curl -X POST http://localhost:45445/_mock/payments/pay_123456789012/overdue
curl -X POST http://localhost:45445/_mock/payments/pay_123456789012/fail
curl -X POST http://localhost:45445/_mock/payments/pay_123456789012/refund \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"value": 50.00}'

Fidelity: proven, not promised

The business rules were captured from the real Asaas sandbox and are checked field by field by a parity suite:

bun run test:parity

tools/capture.ts runs the scenarios against the real Asaas and records the answers in tests/golden/; the suite replays them against the simulator and compares everything that is not legitimately volatile, down to the exact set of keys of each object. The capture knocked down 20 rules deduced from the documentation, among them: a subscription creates its first charge at once; the card installment fee comes off the total and the division truncates (R$ 350 in 12x is R$ 1,20 of fee per installment, not R$ 1,21); installment n credits at D+32×n; the discount field is limitDate, which the specification does not declare.

The calls a real application makes to Asaas were also exercised against the simulator and compared key by key with the sandbox, which found six holes a contract suite could not see (the Pix QR code, installments through POST /v3/payments, paying with an already tokenized card…). See progress.md.

To capture again with your own sandbox key (the script refuses any key that does not start with $aact_hmlg_):

ASAAS_SANDBOX_API_KEY='$aact_hmlg_…' bun run capture
bun run test:parity

Development

bun install
bun run codegen        # OpenAPI spec → TypeBox and the manifest of operations
bun run db:generate    # migrations from the Drizzle schema
bun run dev
bun test
bun run e2e            # boots the server, pays a Pix charge, waits for PAYMENT_RECEIVED
bun run spec:diff      # did Asaas change the API?
